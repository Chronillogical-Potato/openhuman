# Why Remote Teams Still Struggle With Handoffs

Remote work is no longer an experiment. As of 2021, roughly 25% of all paid
workdays in the United States were done from home, and most forecasts at the
time expected that number to keep climbing.

Yet the handoff — the moment one person's work becomes another person's input —
is still where remote teams lose the most time.

## The cost of a bad handoff

A widely cited study found that knowledge workers spend about 60% of their time
on "work about work": status updates, chasing context, and hunting for files.
See the [Anatomy of Work Index](https://asana.com/resources/anatomy-of-work)
for the methodology.

The situation is worse across time zones. When a handoff misses its window, the
work does not slip by an hour; it slips by a full working day.

![Handoff latency by team size](./images/handoff-latency-chart.png)

## Three things that actually help

1. **Write the handoff, do not say it.** A message in a channel is searchable.
   A call is not.
2. **State the next decision, not the status.** "Blocked on the pricing call"
   is useful. "80% done" is not.
3. **Attach the artifact.** The single most common failure is a handoff that
   references a document nobody linked.

Further reading: our [earlier piece on async standups](https://example.com/blog/async-standups-2019)
covers the mechanics.

## What we are trying next

We are moving to a written-first handoff template for every cross-team task
this quarter. I will report back with numbers.
